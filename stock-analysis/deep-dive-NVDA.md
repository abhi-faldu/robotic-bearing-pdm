## 2. Business & Thesis

NVIDIA designs accelerated-computing platforms — GPUs (Blackwell/now Rubin-class),
CPUs (Grace), networking (NVLink, Spectrum-X, InfiniBand), and the **CUDA** software
stack that locks in developers. It is the primary arms dealer of the AI build-out.

**Bull thesis:** Structural AI-infrastructure capex super-cycle; full-stack
rack-scale systems expand TAM and per-system ASP; CUDA moat keeps switching costs
high; guidance ($91B next Q) implies the cycle is still accelerating, not digesting.

**Bear thesis:** Hyperscaler capex is the whole game — any pause in AI ROI scrutiny
hits orders hard; custom ASICs (see §3) chip at merchant-GPU share; China export
restrictions remove a large addressable market; the stock discounts years of growth.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Vector | Threat level |
|---|---|---|
| **AMD** (MI3xx) | Merchant GPU alternative | Rising, still #2 |
| **Broadcom / Marvell** | Custom ASICs for hyperscalers | Structural — these are also in your watchlist |
| Hyperscaler in-house | Google TPU, AWS Trainium/Inferentia, MS Maia | Slow erosion of merchant share |
| Intel | Gaudi accelerators | Laggard |

The key tension: NVIDIA wins the *merchant* market decisively, but the largest
buyers are simultaneously building **custom silicon** (AVGO/MRVL beneficiaries).
NVIDIA's counter is to sell the *network and the rack*, where its moat (NVLink,
Spectrum-X) is currently widening — exactly what the +199% networking growth shows.

## 5. Catalysts (next 6–12 months)

- **Rubin platform ramp** and next-gen networking attach rates
- Quarterly hyperscaler capex guides (META, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN) — the leading indicator
- Any China export-policy change (upside or downside)
- Sovereign-AI and enterprise-AI demand broadening beyond hyperscalers

## 6. Key Risks

1. **AI-capex digestion / ROI backlash** — the single biggest swing factor.
2. **Customer concentration** — a handful of hyperscalers drive the majority of DC revenue.
3. **Custom-ASIC substitution** at the largest accounts.
4. **Geopolitics / export controls** (China).
5. **Valuation/sentiment** — even great prints can sell off (as this one did).

## 7. Bottom Line

Fundamentally the strongest print in the watchlist: beat-and-raise, accelerating,
with the networking franchise emerging as a second growth engine. The stock's muted
reaction to a huge beat is the tell — **NVDA is now an expectations-management story,
not a fundamentals-doubt story.** The risk is no longer "can they execute" but "what
happens to a priced-for-perfection name when AI-capex growth eventually normalizes."
